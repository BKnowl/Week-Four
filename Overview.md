# Overview on Readings - Week Four
___
#### Dennis - Getting it Right and Getting it Wrong in Digital Archaeological Ethics 
- Dennis's discussion of "Black Box" technologies stood out to me. Basically, you put information into software and get a result without necessarily understanding what happened inbetween. I really liked being in control with Polycam, but i was controlling what I captured without really knowing how it processed into a model.
- Her example of digital copies of human remains also raised a deeper question for me. Reburying remains doesn't necessarily remove the digital copies already circulating. We were recording gravestones rather than remains, but could making something digitally permanent conflict with how people want it remembered or accessed? Preserving something sounds positive, but who gets to decide what preservation should involve?
___
#### Huggett - Ethics and Digital Archaeology 
- Honestly, I probably gave my polycam scans more credit than I could justify. Huggett calls this "Automation bias," where we accept a device's results because we trust the technology. Seeing the model come together was really rewarding, and I think that excitement made it easy to overlook parts I'd missed.
- I also liked that Huggett spreads responsibility across people collecting data and those designing and programming tools. During the session, moving too quickly or missing a section, to me felt like I was just learning, but once that scan gets used by someone else, these mistakes could affect their interpretation. For me, that makes recording how I produced the model and where I struggled part of the work itself
